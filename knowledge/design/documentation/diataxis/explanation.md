# Explanation — Understanding-Oriented Documentation

Explanation is a **discursive treatment** of a subject that permits **reflection**. It is always **understanding-oriented** — deepening and broadening the reader's understanding of a subject and bringing clarity, light, and context.

## Core Identity

Explanation is characterised by its **distance from the active concerns of the practitioner**. It doesn't have direct implications for what they do or their work. It may be less *urgent* than the other three modes, but it is no less *important*.

> Explanation is the only kind of documentation that it makes sense to read in the bath.

### Explanation and Understanding

Understanding seals together the other components of mastery of a craft and makes it safely our own. Without explanation, the practitioner's knowledge is loose, fragmented, and fragile — their exercise of it is *anxious*.

Explanation doesn't replace the other modes. It forms the web that helps hold everything together.

### Alternative Names

Explanation documentation does not need to be called "Explanation". Common alternatives include:

- Discussion
- Background
- Conceptual guides
- Topics

The word *explanation* (from Latin *explicare* — to unfold) refers to the revelation of what is hidden in the folds, bringing to light things that were implicit or obscured.

## Key Principles

### Make Connections

Help weave a web of understanding by making connections to other things — even to things outside the immediate topic, if that helps.

### Provide Context

Provide background and context: explain *why* things are so — design decisions, historical reasons, technical constraints. Draw implications and mention specific examples.

### Talk About the Subject

Explanation guides are *about* a topic in the sense that they are *around* it. Even the names should reflect this — you should be able to place an implicit *about* in front of each title:

- *About user authentication*
- *About database connection policies*

Topics for discussion include:
- The bigger picture
- History
- Choices, alternatives, possibilities
- Reasons and justifications

### Admit Opinion and Perspective

All human activity and knowledge is steeped in opinion, beliefs, and thoughts. Explanation can and must consider alternatives, counter-examples, or multiple different approaches to the same question.

Think of explanation as *discussion* — discussions can weigh up contrary opinions.

### Keep Explanation Closely Bounded

Explanation tends to absorb other things. Resist the urge to include instruction or technical description — documentation already has other places for those. Letting them creep in interferes with the explanation itself and hides them from the places where they belong.

## Language of Explanation

Explanation uses **discursive language** to explain, offer judgements, and weigh alternatives:

- *"The reason for x is because historically, y…"*
- *"W is better than z, because…"*
- *"An x in system y is analogous to a w in system z. However…"*
- *"Some users prefer w (because z). This can be a good approach, but…"*
- *"An x interacts with a y as follows:…"*

The tone is reflective, contextual, and open to multiple perspectives.
